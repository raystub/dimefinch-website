Teacher Guide

Free lesson plans for grades 2–6 that pair with the Dimefinch app.

Each plan matches one Dimefinch world and takes about 15 minutes in class, plus the short in-app lessons. The topics follow the six areas of the National Standards for Personal Financial Education: earning income, spending, saving, investing, managing credit and managing risk.

How to use Dimefinch in class

  • Teach, then play: run the activity first, then let students complete the matching in-app lessons.
  • Ages: lessons are labeled for ages 7–10 and 11–12, so you can choose the right level for your class.
  • Privacy: no accounts, ads, chat or tracking. Nothing students do leaves the device.
  • Shared devices: each device holds up to 6 player profiles, each with its own coins, lessons and badges. Add students (or small groups) in For Grown-ups → Children on this device; the app asks “Who’s playing?” when it opens.
  • Read aloud: every lesson card, quiz question and answer can be read aloud, which helps early and emerging readers work on their own. Headphones recommended.
  • What’s free: every lesson for ages 7–10, the first lesson in every world, quizzes, the daily fact, videos and two games are free forever, so every plan below can be taught with the free lessons. Dimefinch Family (optional, for grown-ups) adds the rest of the ages 11–12 lessons, monthly lesson packs, all games, a natural read-aloud voice and progress history.
  • Every day: the free daily money fact and Friday video make good 2-minute warm-ups.

Printables

  • Activity worksheets: tokens, goal cards, price-per-ounce sheet, needs-and-wants cards, jar labels, the snowball chart, a stock tracker, tax price tags and “Real or scam?” cards, with an answer key.
  • Letter to families: a one-page note to send home that explains Dimefinch and how families can use it together. Add your name and class before printing.

🪙 Money Basics

Standard: Earning Income  ·  In the app: What Is Money? · Ways to Earn · Cash, Cards & Digital Money

Goal: Students explain what money is for and name three ways people earn it.

15-minute activity: Classroom market: give each student 3 paper tokens. Half the room “sells” a skill (drawing a name card, telling a joke), the other half buys. Swap roles after 5 minutes. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • Why do people accept money in exchange for things?
  • What jobs do you see people doing in our school?
  • How is paying with a card different from paying with cash?

🐷 Saving Superpowers

Standard: Saving  ·  In the app: Why Save? · Setting Savings Goals · Emergency Funds

Goal: Students set a specific savings goal and calculate how many weeks it takes.

15-minute activity: Goal cards: each student picks an item (with a price you supply), chooses a weekly amount, and divides to find the weeks. Share the fastest and slowest plans and why. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • What makes a goal specific?
  • What would you do if something unexpected cost money?
  • Is it easier to save for something you can picture? Why?

🛒 Smart Spending

Standard: Spending  ·  In the app: Needs vs Wants · Comparing Prices · Ads Want Your Money

Goal: Students sort needs from wants and compare unit prices.

15-minute activity: Grocery flyer hunt: in pairs, find the same item in two sizes and work out the price per ounce. Then sort 10 picture cards into needs and wants. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • Can something be a need for one person and a want for another?
  • What tricks do ads use to make things look exciting?
  • Why might the bigger package not be the better deal?

📊 Budget Builders

Standard: Spending · Saving  ·  In the app: What’s a Budget? · The 50/30/20 Rule · Tracking Your Spending

Goal: Students build a simple budget that includes saving and giving.

15-minute activity: Three jars: give each group $20 in play money to split into SAVE, SPEND and GIVE, then defend their split to the class. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • Why plan before you spend?
  • What happens to a budget when something costs more than expected?
  • Should giving be part of a budget? Why or why not?

🏦 Banks & Interest

Standard: Saving · Investing  ·  In the app: What Banks Do · Interest: Money That Grows · The Magic of Compound Interest

Goal: Students explain interest and see how compound growth builds on itself.

15-minute activity: Snowball chart: start at $100 and add 10% each “year” on the board for 5 years, rounding to cents. Compare with adding a flat $10 a year. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • Why would a bank pay you to keep money there?
  • Why does compound interest grow faster over time?
  • What is the Rule of 72?

📈 Investing Island

Standard: Investing  ·  In the app: Stocks Grow Like a Snowball · What Is Investing? · Stocks & Companies

Goal: Students describe a stock as part-ownership and explain that investments can rise and fall.

15-minute activity: Pick-a-company: each group “buys” a share of a company they know and tracks a made-up price you announce each day for a week. Discuss why prices moved. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • What does it mean to own part of a company?
  • Why is putting all your money in one company risky?
  • Why do people invest for a long time instead of a few days?

💳 Credit & Debt

Standard: Managing Credit  ·  In the app: Borrowing Money

Goal: Students explain that borrowing usually costs extra (interest).

15-minute activity: Borrowing game: students borrow 10 tokens and must repay 12 at the end of class. Discuss when borrowing is worth the extra cost. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • Why do lenders charge interest?
  • What is a good reason to borrow? A not-so-good reason?
  • How can you avoid paying lots of interest?

💼 Paychecks & Taxes

Standard: Earning Income  ·  In the app: Why We Pay Taxes

Goal: Students identify public services paid for by taxes and calculate sales tax.

15-minute activity: Community map: list everything on the walk to school that taxes help pay for (roads, lights, library). Then add 7% tax to three price tags. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • What would our town look like without taxes?
  • Why is sales tax added at the register?
  • Who decides how tax money is spent?

🛡️ Money Safety

Standard: Managing Risk  ·  In the app: Spotting Scams · Safe Online Shopping

Goal: Students recognize common scam warning signs and know to ask a trusted adult.

15-minute activity: Real or scam? Read 6 short messages aloud (“You won a free phone!”, “Your teacher sent the field trip form”). Students vote and explain the warning signs. Worksheet →

Talk about it:

  • What makes a message suspicious?
  • Why should you never share a password?
  • Who can you ask if something online seems strange?

💝 Giving & Sharing

Standard: Spending · Saving  ·  In the app: The Power of Giving

Goal: Students identify ways to give with money, time and kindness.

15-minute activity: Class giving plan: choose one cause together and list three ways the class could help that don’t cost money.

Talk about it:

  • Why do people give to others?
  • How can you help without spending money?
  • How does giving feel?
